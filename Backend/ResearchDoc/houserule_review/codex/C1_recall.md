**D answer.** I cannot name a confirmed same-scene, same-instruction house-rule swap with the joint outcome. The strongest test is a crossover where **each previously forbidden object becomes permitted and each previously permitted object becomes forbidden**. ## Equivalent formulations of CoGround’s rule

CoGround’s \(R=(N,S,T,C=(W,\varphi))\) is **an instance of a conditional deontic policy over grounded actions and execution traces**. Its fields have close precedents. The implementation choices worth comparing are how the policy is compiled from teaching, how missing perceptual facts are handled, and how remaining work is selected.

| Established formulation | Mapping to CoGround |
|---|---|
| **Institutional Grammar ADICO** | Deontic maps to \(N\). Conditions can encode \(S\), object qualifications from \(T\), and the timing guard \(W\). The aIm expresses the regulated action or outcome \(\varphi\) and can name its object. ADICO’s Attribute normally identifies the actor or role. Its Or-else consequence has no direct field in the supplied tuple. |
| **ABAC and access-control policy** | The robot or requester supplies subject attributes, the manipulated entity supplies resource attributes, and the operation supplies the action. Time and local state are environment attributes. A contact decision is close to an authorization check. Obligations and plan-wide effects need policy semantics beyond a simple permit/deny decision. |
| **ODRL and rights-expression languages** | Permission, Prohibition, and Duty map closely to \(N\). The asset, action, and constraints cover much of \(T,\varphi,S\). A constraint or extension can express the execution window. |
| **Production rules and business rules engines** | \(S\land T\) is a fact-matching antecedent. The conclusion attaches a normative status to \(W,\varphi\). Rule priorities, exception links, and missing-fact handling are established implementation patterns. The tuple alone leaves their resolution semantics open. |
| **Defeasible deontic logic and normative MAS** | A conditional obligation or prohibition can be defeated by a higher-priority permission. CoGround’s `exception_of` is a concrete override mechanism. The scope of the override and treatment of incomplete information determine the resulting behavior. |
| **LTL, runtime verification, and constrained planning** | \(W\) selects when the monitor checks \(\varphi\). For example, a scope- and object-qualified prohibition can reject a trace with a forbidden contact. Hard constraints plus a goal for permitted placements express the plan tradeoff. Comparing predictions with observed execution is a familiar planning-and-monitoring distinction. |
| **Contextual integrity and constitutions for agents** | Contextual integrity gives a close analogy for role- and situation-dependent rules over typed entities and actions. A constitution is a standing policy source. Its representation can range from prose to executable constraints. |

The decisive unresolved points are the **source of ownership and attribute facts** in the strongest robotics trials, whether **identical scenes and instructions were crossed with different rules**, and whether any study **isolated decomposition under a held-out combination split**.

## Defensible contribution sentence

> We evaluate whether a robot can follow different resident-taught rules for the same scene and cleanup request, using paired trials with objects held out from teaching and scoring success only when every forbidden object is left untouched and every permitted object is put away correctly.
